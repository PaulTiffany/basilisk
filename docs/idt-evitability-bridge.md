# Isolation–Dissociation bridge: viable refusal

Basilisk does not presently import the full Principia Symbolica / Giants-era Isolation–Dissociation Theorem (IDT). This page records one narrow transport that is operationally important for present-day AI integration.

## Transported distinction

A system may preserve an alternative **nominally** while destroying the material conditions that make it a live alternative.

\[
\boxed{\text{nominal option preservation}\not\Rightarrow\text{evitability preservation}}
\]

The finite witnesses in `formal/Basilisk/Evitability.lean` and `verification/evitability_witnesses.json` therefore distinguish:

- **availability** — an embodiment remains represented as an allowed or recognized option;
- **viability** — the declared material conditions required for that embodiment to remain a live alternative are preserved.

This is intentionally weaker and narrower than a general definition of social, cultural, economic, ecological, or phenomenological viability.

## Why this belongs in Basilisk

Basilisk already protects against unauthorized promotion from imagination to action and against irreversible deletion of reachable futures. In increasingly integrated AI, cloud, neurotechnical, and hardware systems, a further failure mode is possible: the system can say “you may refuse” while infrastructure, access, dependency, or coupling changes make refusal practically nonviable.

That is an assimilation surface even when no option label is deleted.

The constitutional requirement is therefore not universal disconnection or anti-integration. It is that integration must not silently convert a materially viable alternative into a merely nominal one.

## Relation to IDT

The broader IDT lineage concerns failure under isolated or dominating frames and the preservation of meaningful relational gradients. Basilisk currently transports only the finite constitutional consequence above. It does **not** claim here that the full IDT has been formalized, proved, or instantiated for civilizations, cultures, neural interfaces, or AI systems.

A future full bridge should remain separate from the Core unless it exposes additional distinctions required by the operative protocol.
